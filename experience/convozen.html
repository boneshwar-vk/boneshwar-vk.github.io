01 Text to vector

Words become numbers

The sentence is split into tokens, and each token is mapped to a vector the model can work with. Every card keeps its own word, so nothing loses its identity in the transformation.

Four dimensions are shown per token. A real representation has several hundred, so this is an illustrative projection rather than the vector itself.

02 Speaker conditioning

Who is speaking, and how

Text alone does not determine a voice. Speaker and delivery characteristics arrive separately and combine with the text representation to form a single conditioned representation.

Pitch, timbre, energy and speaking rate are illustrative acoustic and speaker features. They do not constitute a complete speaker embedding.

03 Neural generation

Through the generator

The conditioned representation passes through a stack of transformer layers and then a generative decoder, which predicts an acoustic representation rather than audio directly.

The architecture is drawn in the spirit of modern neural TTS systems. It is a simplified illustrative architecture, not a literal diagram of any specific model.

04 Acoustics to speech

A surface, then a signal

The acoustic representation is energy across time and frequency. The ridges running left to right are formants, the resonances that give a vowel its identity. The frequency axis then folds away and what remains is pressure over time, which is what a speaker reproduces.

This surface and the waveform are measured from the same signal the play button uses, so both are views of one piece of data.

About this visualisation

The four stages are one continuous transformation rather than four diagrams. The same word cards become the vectors, the vectors gather into the conditioned representation, and that single object travels the length of the generator before the acoustic surface forms in front of it. Scroll position is the only animation parameter, and the camera moves between chosen vantage points instead of orbiting the scene.

Every object is generated in the browser. The typography is drawn to canvas textures and mapped onto geometry, and the layers, blocks and surfaces are built from primitives. There are no downloaded models and no particle systems standing in for detail.

The acoustic stage is the part with real numbers underneath it. The surface is a short-time Fourier transform on a mel scale, and the waveform is the same signal decimated for display. Playing the sample plays exactly the signal both were measured from.

The earlier stages are conceptual and are labelled where that matters. The vector cards show four representative dimensions of a much larger vector, the speaker features are illustrative rather than a complete embedding, and the generator is a simplified architecture rather than a specific published model.
